About the database

The Presidential Clemency Database is a research project of the Institute for Corporate Governance + Ethics. It brings every pardon and commutation since 1989 into a single, consistently classified record so that researchers, journalists and policymakers can study how clemency is used, and in particular how often it reaches people convicted of white-collar offenses.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a pardon and a commutation?

A pardon forgives a federal conviction and restores certain rights. A commutation shortens or ends a sentence but leaves the conviction in place.

Why are some grants left out of the white-collar share?

Mass actions, such as the Jan. 6 proclamation and the Obama drug commutations, can outnumber a term's individual grants many times over. Leaving them out allows a like-for-like comparison. See Methodology, section 5.

Does the database cover state clemency?

No. It covers federal clemency granted by the president only.

How often is the data updated?

The team checks the DOJ Office of the Pardon Attorney for new grants at least once a month and records each check on the change log.

Request the data

The full dataset, including every record, the summary tables and the data issues log, is available on request. Email ICG@iu.edu with your name, affiliation and how you plan to use the data.

Field definitions, the change log and the suggested citation are on the Methodology page.
